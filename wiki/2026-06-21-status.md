## 1. Offline learning pipeline — CONFIRMED RELIABLE

**Architecture** ([replay-architecture.md](../replay-architecture.md)): a deterministic
observer shadow re-runs `Autopilot` from reconstructed partial-information events;
its whiteboard is the data source. A separate god-view whiteboard measures perception
quality but never mutates robot-side state (the two-whiteboard rule).

**Fidelity is proven, not assumed:**

  mismatches** at `checks=745534` (Aggressive) and `checks=3315293` (Fire) in
  [battle-summary.txt](../build/battle-summary.txt). This is the load-bearing
  guarantee: the observer got the robot's *exact* experience.
- **Layer 1 (spatial/kinematic)** — **0 spatial mismatches**.
- **Layer 4 (energy accounting)** — 2751/2751 and 5646/5646 checks **100%**.

**Corpus exists and is integrity-checked:** a fresh 10-round producer run (seed 12345,

label budget **186,597** real dejavu waves (targeting), **171,257** incoming waves
(movement). The larger 50-round baseline ([ML-intuition.md](ML-intuition.md), ~6.7 GB,

## 2. Online scan + fire detection — CONFIRMED (with nuance)

**Radar/scan:** `NarrowLockRadar` keeps a tight 1v1 lock. Scan coverage is
96.3–99.5% with median ticks-since-scan = 1 across all five reference opponents
(fresh §B6). Solid.

**Incoming-fire detection** ([TheirWaveTracker](../core/src/main/java/cz/zamboch/autopilot/core/features/TheirWaveTracker.java)):
scan-to-scan opponent energy drop, adjusted for wall-hit damage and inactivity-zap,
classified into fire power [0.1, 3.0]. The reported "detection rate" is
`robotSideFires / godViewFires` — a **count ratio, not a fraction of true fires**
([GodViewQualityValidator](../pipeline/src/main/java/cz/zamboch/autopilot/pipeline/GodViewQualityValidator.java)),
and its god-view denominator can itself miss fires. Realized values
([battle-summary.txt](../build/battle-summary.txt)): **Fire 95.7%, BeepBoop 100%,
Aggressive 90.3%, Walls 136.8%, Crazy 156.6%**.

The **>100% values prove the metric is not a blind-detector accuracy**: against clean
shooters detection is ~**95–100%**; the deviations (both below and above 100%) are
dominated by **energy-bookkeeping confounds** — ram/wall damage overlapping the fire
drop (Aggressive), the inactivity-zap leak inflating counts (Walls/Crazy), and god-view
resolver tick-aliasing (BeepBoop, since fixed). The radar/scan layer reliably
*perceives* shots; the residual error is in disambiguating energy drops. My earlier
"~90%" framing understated this.

**Caveats:**
- Confound-driven mis-attribution still propagates to movement that consumes these
  waves; the error is worst on ram-heavy / erratic-energy bots.
- `their-waves.csv` has ~0.01% unresolved/NaN break rows (round-end censoring, §A4) —
  not missing at random.

## 3. VCS gun, online learning — PARTIALLY CONFIRMED

**Mechanically working:**
- [VcsStore](../core/src/main/java/cz/zamboch/autopilot/core/VcsStore.java) is a
  compact `int[5][5][3][31]` (distance × lateral-velocity × lag-1 dodge-context × GF)
  = 2325 bins, with lag-1 → aggregate fallback for cold start.
- Learns **online**: resolved real waves increment bins; persisted to
  `robot/data/vcs.dat` keyed by opponent FNV-1a hash, accumulating across rounds and
  battles ([Autopilot.java](../robot/src/main/java/cz/zamboch/Autopilot.java)).
  Observer instances load but **never persist** (correct separation).
- [ModelSelector](../core/src/main/java/cz/zamboch/autopilot/core/ModelSelector.java)
  provides a regret-windowed multi-model competition framework — but only `VcsStore`
  is currently wired, so it is a no-op selector today.

**The competitiveness gap (grounded, fresh §C4/§G1):**
- Shadow/floor gun hit rate: **8.9–10.0%** depending on opponent.
- Head-on (zero-learning) baseline: **9.6–11.5%**.
- Best-static ceiling: **10.4–11.6%**.
- In-game hero hit rate: **7.4–8.6%** (but fired at self-selected ticks — unpaired,
  trap #7, so not directly comparable).

→ On fresh data the VCS gun is **at or slightly below the head-on baseline for every
opponent** (e.g. ScalarR 9.97% floor vs 10.72% head-on; BeepBoop 9.39% vs 9.64%). GF
distributions are flat (entropy ≈ 5.49 bits; top-1 bin mass ~3%), so these opponents
are genuinely hard to hit, but the best-static ceiling (~10.4–11.6%) shows real
headroom the current gun is not capturing. This re-confirms the baseline conclusion
on an independent 10-round run.

**Other grounded facts for planning context (fresh):**
- Strongest GF predictor by far is `our_fire_lateral_velocity` (RF impurity 0.66 /
  permutation 0.43, η² 0.13); distance/advancing-velocity add little (§C7/§E3).
- GF lag-1 autocorrelation is high (0.69) → sequence/pattern models can beat
  memoryless VCS (§C8).
- Most opponents are stationary after bias correction; `jk.mega.DrussGT` shows the
  only non-zero adaptation (KL 0.104, CI excludes 0) — weaker than the 50-round
  baseline (0.377) because fewer rounds reveal less drift → recency-weighting still
  matters there (§F2).
- Opponent strength (§G2, reference-bot round-robin — **Autopilot excluded by design**):
  `kc.mega.BeepBoop` is strongest (0 losses to peers), `aaa.r.ScalarR` weakest (2/6),
  the rest mid. This ranks targeting difficulty and is **not** Autopilot's own record
  (Autopilot is 0/10 vs BeepBoop per battle-summary.txt).

## 4. Movement — context (not in your claim, but part of baseline)

Baseline `OrbitMovementStrategy` only; no wave surfing yet. Defensive label data
exists (`their-waves.csv`, `their_break_gf` / `their_hit_us`) and dodge profiles are
flat/high-entropy (§D1), i.e. movement is already hard to hit but is **not
model-driven**. Fresh hit-us rate 8.0–9.8%, worst vs `kc.mega.BeepBoop` (9.79%, rising
to 14.1% under high-power ≥1.5 bullets — §D2).
